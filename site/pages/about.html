About Eightfold Helper

A free, independent aid for reflecting on the Noble Eightfold Path, grounded in the early Buddhist discourses.

What it is, and isn't

  • A reflection aid. It helps you notice which factors of the path you might give attention to, and offers simple practices, each tied to a passage from the discourses.
  • Not a measure of spiritual progress. No questionnaire can measure that. Treat the wheel as a mirror for today, not a verdict.
  • Not a teacher. It doesn't replace a teacher, a community or study of the texts, and it doesn't speak for any Buddhist tradition or organisation.
  • Not medical or psychological advice. If you're struggling, please talk to a doctor or someone you trust.

Where the words come from

All {{quote_count}} quotations are Bhikkhu Sujato's English translations, published by SuttaCentral and dedicated to the public domain (CC0). They're taken word for word from SuttaCentral's translation data and checked against it every week. See every source.

The self-check statements follow the definitions of each factor in the “Analysis” discourse (SN 45.8). The chapter commentary and the practices are this site's own, written to be plain and modest. They haven't been reviewed by a teacher; if you see a mistake or something that misrepresents the teachings, please say so.

How the self-check works

  • Quick check: a factor you tick counts as steady; the unticked ones are suggested as starting points, up to three, in the path's traditional order.
  • Full check: each of the {{statement_count}} statements is rated from 1 (rarely) to 5 (almost always). A factor's score is the average of its statements. The two lowest, below 4.5, are suggested as starting points; ties go to the factor listed first, since the discourses say right view comes first.
  • The wheel shows each factor as a spoke of the Dharma wheel, longer for higher scores, coloured by its group: wisdom, ethics or meditation.
